# Dengue — HorizonCare Knowledge Entry

## Topic
Dengue and severe dengue

## Summary
Dengue is a viral infection caused by the dengue virus (DENV).
It is transmitted to humans mainly through the bites of infected
mosquitoes, particularly Aedes aegypti.

Many dengue infections cause mild illness or no symptoms.
When symptoms occur, they may include high fever, severe headache,
pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pains, nausea, vomiting,
swollen glands, and rash.

Most people with symptomatic dengue recover within 1–2 weeks.
Symptoms alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

## Warning Signs of Severe Dengue
Severe dengue can develop and may require urgent hospital care.
Warning signs include:
- Severe abdominal pain
- Persistent vomiting
- Rapid breathing
- Bleeding gums or nose
- Fatigue or restlessness
- Blood in vomit or stool
- Extreme thirst
- Pale and cold skin
- Weakness

These warning signs require immediate medical assessment.
A decrease in fever does not necessarily mean the person is
recovering.

## Diagnosis and Safety
Clinical assessment and appropriate laboratory testing are
important for diagnosis and management.
